# Historical source audit

Reviewed 2026-10-24. This is an availability audit; downloadable files are not evidence of complete seasons. The verified 2015–16 onward reconstruction remains the baseline. No subscription, trial, account, or paid API request was initiated.

A $5 BBall Index pilot would evaluate published model benchmarks, not recover missing defensive assignments.

## Interpretation across eras

Retain competition and season on every record; do not combine regular season, playoffs, play-in and cup finals into season totals by default. The beginning of a statistical category does not prove each provider row recorded it. Missing early records, defunct teams and survival bias prevent treating sparse game sums as official league totals or standings.

Rebounds, minutes, starts, defensive counting statistics and turnovers have different recording histories; historical availability rules are conservative lower bounds, followed by row-level checks. Three-point statistics before the NBA three-point era are not applicable. See [NBA statistical conventions](https://www.nba.com/stats/help/faq).

Rule context belongs alongside comparisons: the 1954–55 introduction of the 24-second clock; the three-point era and temporarily shortened line; zone/illegal-defense and hand-check changes; later offensive-rebound clock resets; expansion and franchise moves; lockouts and the pandemic; changing playoff structures. These alter opportunity and selection. [NBA season history](https://www.nba.com/news/history-season-review-1954-55), [current clock rule](https://official.nba.com/rule-no-7-24-second-clock/), [2011–12 rule changes](https://pr.nba.com/nba-board-of-governors-approves-five-rules-changes-for-2011-12-season/).

Observed possession counts require complete events under the recorded rules; box-score possession formulas are estimates and must never substitute for reconstructed possessions. Per-game rates use the observed covered-game denominator. Era-relative league averages require an adequately covered league population and are not generated from sparse early archives. Tracking-era data are metric-specific; optical tracking availability does not imply legal access to trajectories.
